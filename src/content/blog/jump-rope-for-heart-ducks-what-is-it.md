**Short answer:** Jump Rope for Heart is a school-based fundraising program run by the American Heart Association (AHA). Students collect donations for heart health research and earn prizes — including rubber ducks — based on how much they raise. The program has been running since 1978 and has raised hundreds of millions of dollars.

Here's the full story.

## What Is Jump Rope for Heart?

Jump Rope for Heart (sometimes called "Kids Heart Challenge") is a school program that combines physical activity with fundraising. Students learn jump rope skills in PE class while raising money for the AHA.

The program's goals are:

1. **Teach kids jump rope** as a lifelong fitness skill
2. **Raise money** for cardiovascular research and education
3. **Build heart-healthy habits** early

## What Are the Ducks?

The rubber ducks are one of the program's **fundraising incentive prizes**. Students who raise certain dollar amounts can earn prizes, and the duck is one of the most popular — especially for younger students.

The specific prizes change year to year and school to school, but common tiers look like:

- **$5–10 raised**: Small prize (bracelet, stickers, small toy)
- **$15–25 raised**: Rubber duck
- **$50+ raised**: Larger prizes (T-shirt, sports equipment)
- **$100+**: Top-tier prizes (sometimes including larger plush toys or electronics)

The duck isn't the point — it's an incentive to get kids engaged with the program.

## How the Program Works

A typical Jump Rope for Heart event runs like this:

1. **AHA representative contacts the school** and provides materials.
2. **PE teacher introduces the program** in class.
3. **Students collect donations** from family and friends (in person or online).
4. **Event day**: Students participate in a jump rope event (often during PE class).
5. **Prizes are distributed** based on total funds raised.

The AHA provides:

- Educational materials about heart health
- Fundraising envelopes and online platforms
- Prizes (including the ducks)
- Lesson plans and event guides

## How Much Money Has It Raised?

Jump Rope for Heart has raised over **$1 billion** for the AHA since 1978. It's one of the longest-running school fundraising programs in the US.

The money funds:

- Cardiovascular research
- CPR training in schools
- Public health education
- Advocacy for heart-healthy policies

## Why Does This Matter for Jump Rope Culture?

The program has introduced jump rope to **millions of American kids** who otherwise might never have tried it. Many competitive jumpers and CrossFit athletes first learned the skill in a Jump Rope for Heart event.

It's also one of the few school programs that explicitly ties physical activity to a cause — teaching kids that fitness and community service go together.

## How to Get Your School Involved

If you're a parent or teacher interested in the program:

1. **Contact your local AHA office** or visit the AHA's Kids Heart Challenge page.
2. **Talk to your PE teacher or principal** about hosting an event.
3. **Register online** — the AHA provides a fundraising portal for students.

The program is free for schools to participate in — the AHA covers the cost of materials and prizes.

## What If You Just Want the Ducks?

The ducks themselves aren't sold directly. They're only available as fundraising prizes.

If you want a rubber duck for your kid's collection, you have a few options:

- **Participate in the program** through your school
- **Buy from third-party sellers** (Amazon, etc.) — but these are not the official AHA ducks
- **Look for past-year ducks** at garage sales or online resale

The official program ducks change every year, so older ones can become collectibles.

## The Bottom Line

Jump Rope for Heart ducks are a fundraising incentive for a school program that has raised over $1 billion for heart health research. The program teaches kids jump rope, builds heart-healthy habits, and connects fitness to community service.

If you're a parent or teacher, it's worth checking whether your school participates. If you're an athlete, it's worth knowing this is where a lot of your peers first picked up a rope.
